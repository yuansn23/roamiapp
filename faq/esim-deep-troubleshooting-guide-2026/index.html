🛠️ 16 In‑Depth Troubleshooting Steps

#1 eSIM Stuck on “Activating” for More Than 10 Minutes

Symptom: After adding an eSIM, the status bar shows “Activating…” with no change for over 10 minutes.

Root Causes:

  • Carrier’s SM‑DP+ server address is incorrect or unreachable.
  • Enterprise MDM has enabled the AllowESIMModification restriction, blocking new eSIM additions.
  • A previously incomplete eSIM profile conflicts with the new request.

Solutions:

  1. Check MDM restrictions: Go to Settings > General > VPN & Device Management. Look for a profile that prohibits eSIM modification. If present, ask your admin to temporarily remove it.
  2. Clear residual profiles: Go to Settings > Cellular. If you see a gray, unclickable “Incomplete” entry, restart your phone and try again.
  3. Manual entry (bypass QR errors): Request the SM‑DP+ address and activation code from your carrier. Then go to Settings > Cellular > Add eSIM > Enter Details Manually and fill in the fields. Leave the confirmation code blank.
  4. Enterprise MDM final command: Admins can push the RefreshCellularPlansCommand via MDM to force‑refresh eSIM configuration.

#2 eSIM Installed but Shows “No Service” or “Searching”

Symptom: The eSIM is successfully added, but the status bar shows “No Service” or keeps searching.

Root Causes:

  • The MCC/MNC (carrier code) in the eSIM profile does not match the local cell tower broadcast.
  • A restricted PLMN list was written (common after a failed registration attempt during roaming).
  • A data‑only eSIM is mistakenly set as the voice line.

Solutions:

  1. Manually select network: Settings > Cellular > tap the eSIM > Network Selection → turn off “Automatic”, manually pick a different carrier (e.g., if on China Mobile, select China Unicom), wait for failure, then switch back to “Automatic” to force re‑registration.
  2. Field Test diagnosis: Dial *3001#12345#* to enter Field Test mode. Go to Serving Cell Info and check whether the PLMN matches your eSIM’s carrier. If not → roaming agreement missing, you need a local eSIM.
  3. Data‑only eSIM settings: If the plan is data‑only, go to Settings > Cellular > Cellular Data, select this eSIM, and turn off “Allow Cellular Data Switching” to prevent the system from falling back to your primary number.

#3 eSIM Transfer Fails (Old Device → New Device, or Android ↔ iPhone)

Symptom: When using iOS 26’s “Transfer from Android” or “Transfer from Nearby iPhone”, you get an error saying “Transfer Failed” or “Session Expired”.

Root Causes:

  • Cross‑platform scenario: Android’s Wi‑Fi security scanning blocks the broadcast; iPhone’s “Local Network” permission is missing.
  • Same platform scenario: The old device removed the eSIM but did not wait for server unbinding (requires at least 5 minutes); the new device tries to transfer immediately and is rejected.
  • Carrier limits the number of transfers (some prepaid eSIMs allow only 1 transfer).

Solutions:

  1. Android preparation: Turn off “Wi‑Fi security scan” and “Auto‑connect suggestions” (usually in Wi‑Fi advanced settings).
  2. iPhone side permission: Settings > Privacy & Security > Local Network → ensure that the “Settings” app is granted permission (new requirement in iOS 26).
  3. Wait for unbinding: After performing Settings > Cellular > Delete eSIM on the original device, wait at least 5 minutes before starting the transfer on the new device.
  4. Reset transfer quota: If you see “Transfer limit reached”, contact your carrier to reset the profile’s write quota.

#4 “Carrier Lock” Prevents Adding eSIM

Symptom: When trying to add a non‑home carrier eSIM, you see “SIM Restrictions” or “Carrier Lock does not allow this eSIM”.

Root Causes:

  • A locked device can only use eSIM profiles from its locked carrier.
  • Some carriers allow automatic provisioning via eSIM Carrier Activation without a physical card.
  • Failure for users abroad: the carrier requires first‑time eSIM activation to occur within the home network coverage area.

Solutions:

  1. Check lock status: Settings > General > About → see if “Carrier Lock” says “SIM Restricted”.
  2. Home‑network activation from abroad: If you are outside your home country, ask a friend/home contact with the same Apple ID to log into the device (requires iOS 26’s “Allow Remote Activation” feature, supported by some carriers) and trigger activation remotely.
  3. No workaround: If your carrier does not support remote activation, you must go back to your home country to add the eSIM for the first time; after that, roaming will work globally.

#5 eSIM Causes FaceTime / iMessage Activation Failure

Symptom: After installing an eSIM, FaceTime or iMessage stays in “Waiting for Activation” or shows “Activation Failed”.

Root Causes:

  • iMessage registration requires the phone number associated with the eSIM to send an international SMS to Apple’s activation server.
  • Some data‑only eSIMs do not have SMS capability, or the carrier has blocked the SMS function on that number segment.
  • Enterprise MDM profiles with Force Apple ID Login Restriction can block automatic binding between iMessage and the eSIM.

Solutions:

  1. Manually trigger authentication: Using that eSIM line, send an ordinary SMS to any number and make a phone call (even 1 second). Both services need to activate carrier‑side authentication.
  2. Reset SMS center: Dial ##002# (delete all call forwarding), then dial ##004# (reset SMS center). Wait 10 seconds, then restart.
  3. Manually select the line: Settings > Messages > Send & Receive, try to manually check the eSIM number. If not selectable, repeat step 1.
  4. Enterprise devices: Ask your MDM admin whether an Apple ID restriction policy is enabled, and ask to whitelist the eSIM’s number.

#6 Travel eSIM Shows Signal but No Data (Already Paid)

Symptom: You bought and activated a travel eSIM. It shows signal bars, but you cannot access the internet (browser says “Not connected”).

Root Causes:

  • APN misconfiguration: Most travel eSIMs require manually entering an APN (e.g., internet, cmnet, or a carrier‑specific value). Leave username/password blank unless explicitly provided. Wrong values cause authentication failure.
  • iOS 26’s new routing strategy: When your primary eSIM has data roaming enabled, the system may incorrectly route the data tunnel through the primary line.

Solutions:

  1. Manually set APN: Settings > Cellular > tap the eSIM > Cellular Data Network → enter the APN value provided by the carrier (common: internet, globaldata, 3internet). Leave username and password blank unless the carrier explicitly gives them.
  2. Turn off roaming on primary line: Settings > Cellular > Primary (home number) → turn off “Data Roaming”. Then go to Settings > Cellular > Cellular Data and select the travel eSIM.
  3. Force Low Data Mode: Turn on “Low Data Mode” for the travel eSIM to force it to use its own APN instead of the system default.
  4. DNS pollution test: Use a terminal app (or directly type in Safari address bar) to visit http://8.8.8.8. If you get a response but domain names do not resolve → manually change DNS to 1.1.1.1.

#7 After Enterprise MDM Remote Erase, eSIM Cannot Be Reinstalled

Symptom: An admin used the MDM RefreshCellularPlansCommand or remotely deleted an eSIM. The device still shows the eSIM but has no signal, and rescanning the QR code fails.

Root Causes:

  • Remote erase only removed the eSIM’s user data, but the EID mapping between the eUICC chip and the baseband remains.
  • The device thinks the eSIM still exists and rejects a new profile overlay.

Solutions:

  1. Restore from backup (if you have a .pkpass file): Settings > Cellular > Add eSIM > Use QR Code → select the previously exported .pkpass certificate file.
  2. Reset network settings: Settings > General > Transfer or Reset iPhone > Reset > Reset Network Settings. This will not delete the eSIM but will reset the eUICC/baseband mapping. After that, reinstall the profile.
  3. Force erase via Apple Configurator: On a Mac, open Apple Configurator, connect the device, select it, then go to Actions > Advanced > Erase all eUICC profiles. This requires the device to be in recovery mode.

#8 Second eSIM Has No Signal in Dual eSIM Scenario (Hardware Conflict)

Symptom: One eSIM (e.g., Verizon) is active. After adding a second eSIM (e.g., T‑Mobile), the second eSIM has no signal. When you disable the first eSIM, the second one gets signal immediately.

Root Causes:

  • When the first eSIM belongs to a CDMA carrier (e.g., older Verizon plans), the baseband locks into CDMA voice mode. The second eSIM can only use LTE/5G data and cannot support CDMA voice fallback simultaneously.
  • Rare edge case: both eSIMs use the same frequency band (e.g., Band 71), but the baseband firmware does not support dual‑SIM dual‑active (DSDA).

Solutions:

  1. Downgrade network mode: For the eSIM with no signal, go to Settings > Cellular > tap the eSIM > Voice & Data → change from “5G Auto” to “LTE”, then back to 5G. This forces the baseband to reallocate resources.
  2. Swap primary/secondary: Settings > Cellular > Cellular Data – select the second eSIM as the data card. Default Voice Line – select the first eSIM. Let the data card take priority for 5G.
  3. Change CDMA plan: If the first eSIM is CDMA and you can change it, switch to a GSM/5G‑compatible plan (e.g., Verizon’s “5G Start” plan).

#9 After Deleting eSIM, Carrier Still Charges + Cannot Reactivate

Symptom: You tapped “Delete eSIM” on your phone. Next month you are still billed for that number, and rescanning the original QR code says “Expired”.

Root Causes:

  • Deleting eSIM ≠ canceling the plan. Your subscription with the carrier remains active; only the device‑side profile is gone.
  • This creates an “orphaned state” – the profile is still active but not linked to any EID, so the carrier cannot re‑issue it.

Solutions:

  1. Correct process: First contact your carrier to cancel the number/plan, get confirmation, then perform “Delete eSIM” on the phone.
  2. After accidental deletion: Provide your EID (Settings > General > About > EID) to the carrier and ask them to re‑issue the eSIM profile. Exact phrasing: “Please perform a profile re‑issue on the same number.” After re‑issuance, the original QR code will be valid for 30 minutes.
  3. Stop overcharging: Ask the carrier to stop billing from the deletion date and refund any overcharged amount.

#10 eSIM Causes Device Update / Restore Failure (DFU Error 53 or 4013)

Symptom: When trying to restore your iPhone via DFU mode, you get error 53 or 4013, and the restore process stops.

Root Causes:

  • A corrupted eSIM profile stored in the eUICC causes the baseband to hang during profile verification, blocking the restore.
  • This often happens when a user forcibly aborts the eSIM activation process, leaving a half‑written profile.

Solutions:

  1. Delete before restoring (if device can boot): If your device still boots, go to Settings > Cellular > Delete all eSIMs, then enter DFU restore.
  2. Already unbootable: Use Apple Configurator on a Mac. Connect the device, enter recovery mode. Select Advanced > Ignore eSIM lock (new option in iOS 26), then perform the firmware restore.
  3. After restore: Contact your carrier to re‑download the eSIM profile – the number remains the same.

#11 eSIM Activation Asks for “Confirmation Code” – User Doesn’t Know What That Is

Symptom: After scanning the carrier’s QR code, the system pops up an “Enter Confirmation Code” screen. The user tries leaving it blank, entering 0000, or entering the SIM PIN – all fail.

Root Causes:

  • The eSIM activation code format is LPA:1$SM-DP+address$activationCode$confirmationCode. The confirmation code is an optional carrier field.
  • Users often confuse “confirmation code” with “PIN code” or “PUK code”. PIN is the local password for a physical SIM, unrelated to eSIM.
  • Some enterprise‑batch eSIMs require a confirmation code to proceed.

Solutions:

  1. Try leaving it blank first: Just tap “Continue”. If that fails →
  2. Look in the original email: Below the QR code or in the email body, carriers often write Confirmation Code: XXXX or Confirmation code：XXXX.
  3. Use the correct phrasing when contacting support: Do not say “PIN code”. Say “I need the Confirmation Code for eSIM activation.”
  4. Manual entry mode: If the carrier gives you the SM-DP+ address and activation code but no confirmation code, choose Enter Details Manually and fill only the first two fields, leaving confirmation code blank. If it still asks for a confirmation code, then the profile enforces it – you must obtain it from the carrier.

#12 iPad Cannot Join iPhone’s “Shared Number” (Number Share Failure)

Symptom: On your iPad, you go to Settings > Cellular Data > Add to iPhone Number but you do not see your iPhone’s number, or tapping it does nothing.

Root Causes:

  • This feature requires the iPhone’s plan to be Postpaid. Prepaid almost never supports it.
  • The iPhone and iPad must be signed into the exact same Apple ID (iCloud sync alone is not enough; the primary account must match).
  • The carrier must explicitly support eSIM companion devices, and both devices must be linked in the carrier’s database.

Solutions:

  1. Confirm plan type: Contact your carrier and ask, “Does my number support multi‑device eSIM sharing (Number Share)?”
  2. First add the device on the iPhone: Settings > Cellular > tap the number > Add New Device (this option appears only if the carrier supports it). A pairing code will be generated; enter it on the iPad.
  3. Force refresh Apple ID credentials: On the iPad, go to Settings > Apple ID > Sign Out (not just signing out of iCloud), then sign back in with the same Apple ID.
  4. eSIM‑only iPad model: Ensure your iPad is an eSIM‑capable model (iPad Pro M4 / iPad Air M2 / iPad mini A17 Pro and later).

#13 “eSIM Profile Expired” or “QR Code Invalid”

Symptom: Immediately after scanning the QR code, you get an error saying “This code has expired” or “Invalid profile”. You have never successfully added it.

Root Causes:

  • eSIM profiles have a lifecycle on the SM‑DP+ server: CREATED → RELEASED → DELETED. If you scan a QR code more than 24–72 hours after receiving it, the profile becomes RELEASED and cannot be downloaded.
  • Some carriers issue single‑use QR codes: even if not expired, scanning it once invalidates it to prevent multi‑use.

Solutions:

  • Do not rescan the same QR code repeatedly. If the first activation fails (e.g., due to network interruption), do not scan again. Instead, go to Settings > Cellular > Add eSIM. The system will prompt “Incomplete eSIM detected”, tap “Continue”.
  • If clearly expired: Contact your carrier and request a re‑issue (new QR code), asking them to set a longer validity (e.g., 7 days).
  • After re‑issue: Activate within 1 hour. It is best to do it immediately upon receiving the new QR code.

#14 Dual SIM: One Number Can Call Out but Cannot Receive Incoming Calls (Other Number Works)

Symptom: On an iPhone with two numbers (eSIM A + eSIM B), A works normally. B can make outgoing calls, but incoming calls to B ring as “unreachable” or go straight to voicemail/busy tone.

Root Causes:

  • The carrier’s core network T‑ADS (Terminating Access Domain Selection) cache is wrong. When the B number recently switched networks (e.g., from 5G to LTE or used Wi‑Fi Calling), the old domain cache remains, causing incoming calls to route to the wrong domain.
  • B might have accidentally enabled “Call Forwarding” to a dead number, or “Silence Unknown Callers” might be blocking all calls.

Solutions:

  1. Check simple settings: Settings > Phone > Silence Unknown Callers → turn off. Settings > Phone > Call Forwarding → check if accidentally turned on.
  2. Force re‑registration: Settings > Cellular > B number → turn off “Enable This Number”, wait 10 seconds, then turn it back on.
  3. Delete all call forwards: Dial ##002#, press call, wait for “Removal completed” message.
  4. Contact carrier to refresh T‑ADS cache: Exact phrasing: “Please clear the domain selection record for this number and reset it to default LTE/5G domain.” Usually takes effect within 30 minutes.

#15 eSIM‑Only Device Cannot Activate Without Wi‑Fi

Symptom: You bought a brand new eSIM‑only iPhone (US model, no physical SIM slot). After turning it on, you cannot connect to any Wi‑Fi, and there is no cellular signal. You are stuck on the “Wi‑Fi Required” screen and cannot proceed.

Root Causes:

  • Apple provides a “Wi‑Fi‑less Activation” feature for eSIM‑only devices (using a special activation network, PLMN like 310‑260). This requires:
    • The device’s location must have coverage from that activation network (usually the original purchase carrier’s network).
    • The device must be unlocked.
    • Local laws must permit it (allowed in the US, not allowed in some EU countries).
  • If any condition is not met, the device gets stuck.

Solutions:

  1. Use a personal hotspot: Use another phone (Android or iPhone) to create a hotspot, and let the new iPhone connect to that hotspot to complete initial setup.
  2. Skip activation via computer: Connect the iPhone to a Mac (Apple Configurator) or PC (iTunes). Select the device, click “Restore” or “Update”. In iOS 26, Apple Configurator has a new option Advanced > Skip Activation Wizard, which directly brings you to the home screen.
  3. Visit a store: If none of the above works, go to an Apple Store or carrier store. They have dedicated activation devices (signal generators) that can force‑activate the device.

#16 Used iPhone Says “eSIM Already Bound to Another Device”

Symptom: After buying a used iPhone, you scan a new eSIM QR code and get an error: “This profile is already bound to another device” or “EID mismatch”.

Root Causes:

  • An eSIM profile is generated with a one‑to‑one binding to the target device’s EID.
  • The previous owner either did not “Delete eSIM” before selling, or they deleted it but the carrier’s backend still marks the profile as active.
  • Your new device’s EID conflicts with the old profile record.

Solutions:

  1. Ask the seller for proof: Ask the seller to go to Settings > General > About on their old device, screenshot the EID, and confirm they performed Settings > Cellular > Delete eSIM.
  2. Contact your carrier to unbind: Provide your new device’s EID and IMEI, and ask them to release the binding with the old EID. Phrase: “Please perform a Profile Release for old EID xxxx to free the number.”
  3. Last resort – new eSIM: If the carrier refuses due to account permission issues, you will need to request a completely new eSIM (i.e., a new number or a fresh QR code).
  4. Enterprise MDM scenario: An MDM admin can use the Delete eSIM command and wait 15 minutes; the new device will then be ready for writing.

✅ Final Advice: How to Avoid Getting Into Troubleshooting States

  • Before activation: Confirm your carrier supports eSIM, your device is unlocked, and the QR code is less than 48 hours old.
  • Before transferring: After deleting the eSIM on the original device, wait at least 5 minutes.
  • Before traveling abroad: After purchasing a travel eSIM, manually set the APN in advance and test the data connection while not roaming.
  • Before selling your device: Delete all eSIMs under Settings > Cellular, and notify your carrier to cancel the associated plans.

This guide is compiled based on Apple official technical documentation (2025‑2026) and real‑user cases. Actual functionality may vary depending on carrier policies, region, and device firmware version. If your issue remains unresolved, contact your carrier and provide your EID and IMEI.
